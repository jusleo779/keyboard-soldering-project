# keyboard-project
Background:

I developed and designed a keyboard using off-the-shelf parts, featuring an AT32 Mega microcontroller and a Tanuki PCB. I learned and applied various skills that interested and excited me, while also developing skills for future work. This project required months of making design choices, developing multiple drafts of 3D models, and soldering the parts with many mistakes along the way. In the end, the project wasn't completed fully due to making many mistakes, but in the process, I learned a lot! 

Overview of Project Planning:
The project planning took about a month and a half researching and learning the required skills to complete the project. This part was lengthy because I wanted to make sure that I had everything I needed from the videos or courses to learn the different skills to the specific parts on the project. It didn't all go to plan as I hoped, but that's a part of learning!

Research:
To start my research, I thought that I could start from scratch, learning PCB design and soldering. But through my research, the pcb design required circuit knowledge that I have yet to learn,      and with restricted time to complete this project, I decided I would focus on learning soldering. This switched my research to finding a project, which led me to this project. Through this project, I researched off-the-shelf parts using my previous knowledge for researching from my Hospital-Induced Delirium project. I researched everything I needed for the project and found parts that would suffice for the project. Throughout the research process, I used specific prompting in ChatGPT to increase the speed of my research for finding essential parts for the project.

Learning:
The main focus of the project was to learn soldering, but in the process of the project, I developed essential knowledge through many failures and mistakes. 

My source of learning for soldering was through tutorials on YouTube, which gave a visual understanding of the correct way to solder parts. Some essential points when soldering are: the temperature of the solder depends on the material, the solder must be coated in the soldering iron before use, and it must touch both metal pieces that you want to solder together before soldering. Although it's good to learn theory, it requires real-world application, which led to many mistakes throughout my soldering. Some things I learned through real-world application:

 1. Soldering parts through a hole is essentially permanent with just a soldering iron. I learned this the hard way, which led to many workarounds throughout the project.

 2. Soldering parts requires less solder than you think, so be careful with how much you use unless you want to clean it up with a solder pump or solder wick.

 3. Soldering Flux paste "cleans" the board and makes the soldering easier

 4. My most critical mistake was not being careful with solder. The microcontroller's USB port fell off due to the heat of the solder touching and heating the microcontroller and the PCB it was connected. When I made this mistake, I tried to reconnect it by soldering it back on, but my limited knowledge made it harder and led to more mistakes.
